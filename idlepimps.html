Pimpin' FAQ
Beginner's Guide

You start with $4,500, 1 hoe, 1 thug, and 4,000 turns in New York City. The first thing to do is sell your starting thug and spend everything on condoms and crack — your hoes need both to stay happy before you can make any real money.

Turn Tricks 8 at a time in the early going. Turning 8 at once is more efficient than turning larger amounts when you're small — you recruit more hoes and thugs per turn spent. Each time you turn, buy more crack, condoms, weed, and beer with what you make. Once you have a solid supply stock, start buying AK-47s: one per thug.

Don't attempt a serious build until you have at least 800–1,000 turns banked. Building too early with too few turns leaves you with a bad thug-to-hoe ratio and makes you an easy target. Patience early on pays off.

Rounds last one week. The pimp with the highest net worth when time runs out wins.

Hoe Happiness

Your hoes need two things to stay happy: condoms and crack. At 1% payout (the standard setting), each hoe needs 10 crack rocks in your stash to stay at 100% happiness. Condoms are consumed every time you turn tricks.

Always keep your hoes at 100% happiness. Any hoe that isn't fully happy can be stolen from you by an enemy pimp with as little as 1 crack rock. Don't give them the opening.

The payout % is the cut your hoes take from each trick. At 100% payout they barely need any crack to stay happy (they're getting paid well), but you make almost nothing. At 1% payout you keep 99% of the money — but you need 10 crack per hoe in stock to keep them satisfied. Always pimp at 1% unless you have a specific reason not to.

One exception: if you want to lower your visible net worth to avoid being targeted, flip to 100% payout temporarily. Your crack supply stays the same but it shows less in your NW since you need less of it.

Thug Happiness

Your thugs need weed and beer to stay at 100% happiness. Neither is consumed over time — once you stock them, they sit in your inventory indefinitely. But you still need enough on hand relative to your thug count, so as your stable grows you'll need to restock both.

Thugs also need 1 AK-47 each to fight at full strength — unarmed thugs are nearly useless in combat. Kevlar Vests aren't mandatory but give your thugs a defensive edge when fighting, making them harder to kill. An unhappy or underequipped thug force will lose fights against a smaller but well-supplied one every time.

Weed, beer, AKs, and Kevlar Vests don't add to your net worth, which makes all four useful for parking cash invisibly. A pimp who just made $100M can dump it all into AKs and vests and look flat on the rankings. Stock up.

Net Worth

Your net worth (NW) is what everyone else uses to size you up and determine if they can attack you:

NW = Cash + (Hoes × $250) + (Thugs × $1,000) + (Rides × $1,000) + (Crack × $2)

The following items do not add to your NW: AK-47s, Kevlar Vests, Weed, Beer, Condoms. These are the smart places to park your wealth so enemies can't see it. A pimp sitting on $50M in cash is a gank target. A pimp who spent that $50M on AKs and weed looks broke.

You can also deliberately lower your NW by selling thugs or crack to get into attack range of a target who'd otherwise be out of reach. This is called diving.

Stealing Hoes & the Crack-u-lator

Stealing hoes is the fastest way to build your stable — far faster than producing hoes by turning tricks. To steal, you send crack to a target's hoes as a bribe. If you send the right amount, their hoes defect and join you.

Visit any pimp's profile and click Crack-u-lator. It will calculate how much crack to send based on their net worth, hoe count, thug count, and ride count. The formula works because crack is the only NW-visible supply — so the difference between what you can account for and their actual NW tells you how much crack they're sitting on.

A few rules of thumb:
— Always kill a target's thugs before trying to steal their hoes. You need a thug-to-hoe ratio below 4:1 before crack stealing becomes effective.
— If you get fewer than 100 hoes on a steal, you sent too much crack. Try about 1/10th the amount next time.
— If a target's hoes are already unhappy (they're low on crack), you can steal from them with just 1 crack rock — you don't even need to kill their thugs first.
— Don't steal hoes if you don't have enough turns to protect them immediately. Stolen hoes with no thugs covering them are just a gift to whoever ganks you next.

Attacks

You can attack any pimp in your city whose NW is between 0.5× and 2× your own. There are four attack types:

Home Invasion — The standard attack. Your thugs storm the target's crib. If you win, you take their cash. Best used against pimps who just turned tricks and are sitting on money (this is called a gank).

Drive-By — Requires lowriders (1 ride per 4 thugs attacking). Drive-bys kill more of the enemy's thugs while losing fewer of your own — roughly a 5:1 kill ratio in your favor. Use this to tear down a fortified target before moving in for hoes or cash.

Steal Hoes — Send crack rocks to bribe the target's hoes. See the Crack-u-lator section above.

Jack Rides — If a target has fewer than 4 thugs per lowrider, their rides are exposed. You can steal them outright. Go get some wheels.

All attacks cost 1 turn. Thug happiness and AK count matter — happy, armed thugs win; unhappy, unarmed thugs die.

Ganking

A gank is attacking a pimp the instant they turn tricks — before they have a chance to spend their cash. When someone turns tricks, their NW jumps visibly because cash shows up in net worth. That window between turning and spending is when they're most vulnerable.

To gank effectively, open multiple browser windows: one watching the target's profile or the attack rankings, one with the attack form pre-loaded, and one with IdleMart ready to spend whatever you take. Refresh constantly. The moment a pimp jumps into your attack range with a suspicious NW spike, hit them immediately.

Just as importantly — spend your own cash the moment you make it. Sitting on money makes you the target.

Cities

You can travel between cities at any time from the Travel page. Cost is based on how many hoes and thugs you bring — any thugs you don't take are lost permanently. Having 1 lowrider per 4 thugs gives you a travel discount.

Each city has a different character:

New York City — The default starting city. Balanced payouts. Lots of players means lots of targets — and lots of people watching you.

London — Cheap to travel to ($30/head). Good for escaping heat or setting up in a quieter city early in a round.

Atlanta — Highest cash payouts when turning tricks. The money city. Most experienced pimps turn here once they're built.

Las Vegas — Best city for recruiting hoes when turning 8s. If you're building your stable by producing rather than stealing, this is your home.

Pimps can only attack each other within the same city. If someone is hunting you, travel to shake them.

Glossary

Gank — Attacking a pimp the moment they turn tricks to steal their cash.
Hoe gank — Stealing a pimp's hoes right after zeroing their thugs, before they can rebuy protection.
Drop / Dropping — Killing all of a pimp's thugs to expose their hoes for stealing.
Zero — Killing all of a pimp's thugs entirely.
Dive / Diving — Selling thugs or crack to lower your own NW and get into range of a target.
Deadnet — What a pimp's NW would be with no thugs: their hoes + crack + rides only. Tells you how juicy a target is after you've zeroed them.
Spinning — Turning tricks (putting your hoes to work).
Thugwhore — A pimp who turns tricks and stacks thugs without attacking much. Usually makes a move near the end of a round.
Crackie — A pimp who stockpiles crack instead of building defensively. Low effort, moderate rank.
Padder — Someone who attacks only to inflate their stats, not to actually compete.
Suicide run — Attacking a gang or pimp to zero them, knowing you won't recover yourself afterward.
Multi — Running more than one account. Bannable offense, no exceptions.
Spread — A spreadsheet or external tool used to pre-calculate crack amounts, deadnets, and NW math. The in-game Crack-u-lator covers most of this.
Turns & Tokens

Turns are the currency of everything you do in Pimp City. Attacking, turning tricks, traveling, stealing hoes — it all costs turns. You accumulate turns automatically: the game awards 40 turns every 10 minutes to all players simultaneously. You can hold a maximum of 4,000 turns at any time — turns above this cap are wasted, so don't let yourself sit at max for long.

Tokens are a special resource that bypasses the 4,000 turn cap entirely. Each token converts to 500 turns when redeemed and stacks on top of whatever you already have. You start each round with 1 token. Redeem them from the Token Manager link in the main menu.

Use tokens strategically. The best time to redeem is right before a big move — when you're about to attempt a major drop, when you need to turn tricks fast after stealing a large stable, or when you're racing someone else who's also trying to build. Don't redeem tokens and then go offline; that's wasted potential.

A note on pacing: the turn cap means the longer you wait to spend turns, the more of the incoming ticks you're silently wasting. Active players who spend turns quickly will always accumulate more total turns over the course of a round than players who hoard.

Gangs

Gangs are crews of up to 12 pimps. Joining a strong gang is one of the best things a new pimp can do — you get coordination, protection, and shared intelligence on who's building and who's vulnerable.

Joining a gang: Only the gang's leader or co-leader can invite you. You can browse Gang Rankings to find active gangs and see who's in them. Once invited, you'll see the offer at the bottom of your main menu to accept or decline. Don't join a gang that's at war with everyone — you inherit their enemies.

Gang dues: The leader sets a dues percentage. Every time you turn tricks, that percentage of your earnings goes to the gang treasury automatically. Dues fund collective operations — buying crack for a coordinated drop, for example. If dues are set high and the gang isn't offering anything in return, that's a red flag.

Gang coordination: At the top level, the game is played as a gang game. Coordinated ganks — multiple members watching the same target simultaneously — are nearly impossible to escape. Coordinated drops — one member drive-bying while another has the crack attack ready — are how big stables get taken down fast. Solo players at high NW are always vulnerable to a coordinated crew.

Gang rules:
— Backstabbing (leaving a gang and attacking former members) is permitted — it's part of the game.
— Rejoining a gang after backstabbing them is against the rules. If you burn a bridge, it stays burned.
— Alliances between gangs are allowed as long as each gang has its own separate leadership. Alliances can't be used to form an effective super-gang that circumvents the 12-member limit.
— Multiple accounts (multis) used to stuff a gang are a ban offense.

Creating a gang: You can create your own gang from the Gang Menu. You'll need a name and to set your dues rate. As leader you can invite members, set a co-leader, and kick members who go inactive or turn on the gang.

The Casino

The Belagi-HOE Casino is one of the most overlooked parts of the game. It's free turns if you play it consistently — and turns are everything.

The game is simple: the dealer reveals a card, you're dealt a card face down, and you guess whether your card is Higher or Lower than the dealer's. Wager turns to play. Win and you double your wager. Lose and it's gone.

Smart play: If the dealer shows a low card (2 through 6), bet Higher — the odds favor you. If the dealer shows a high card (9 through Ace), bet Lower. Middle cards are a coin flip; wager small or skip. There's no memory between rounds so each hand is independent.

The Casino doesn't cost you anything but turns you were going to spend anyway. Make it a habit to run a session whenever you're sitting on turns and waiting for something to happen. Even modest consistent play adds up over a round.

Producing Crack

The Produce Crack page lets your thugs manufacture crack instead of buying it at $10/rock from IdleMart. This costs turns but saves cash — a useful tradeoff when you're building a large crack supply for a drop and don't want to drain your bankroll.

Happy thugs with AK-47s produce more crack per turn than unhappy or unarmed ones. Keep them stocked before running a production session.

The Drop: Full Sequence

Dropping a pimp is the art of demolishing their defenses and stealing their entire hoe stable. Done well it's the fastest way to climb the rankings. Here's the full sequence:

Step 1 — Recon. Open the target's profile and run the Crack-u-lator. Note their thug count, hoe count, and NW. Calculate how much crack you'll need and have it ready to send. Make sure you have enough rides for drive-bys (1 ride per 4 thugs attacking).

Step 2 — Kill the thugs. Use Drive-Bys to tear down their thug count. Drive-bys are more efficient than Home Invasions for pure thug-killing — you lose fewer of your own thugs at roughly a 5:1 kill ratio. Keep attacking until their thug-to-hoe ratio drops below 4:1. At that point their hoes become stealable even if they're happy.

Step 3 — Steal the hoes. The moment their thugs are sufficiently depleted, use the Steal Hoes attack and send your pre-calculated crack amount. If their hoes were already unhappy before you started, you can skip Step 2 entirely — send 1 crack rock and see what you get first.

Step 4 — Turn immediately. Don't sit on a large stolen stable. Every second those hoes are unprotected by your thug count is a window for someone to steal them back from you. Turn tricks right away to build your NW and get your thug ratio back to safe levels. Do this in a different city from where you dropped the target — people nearby were watching.

Step 5 — Spend the cash. You're now visible on the rankings with a much larger NW. Other pimps are watching you the same way you were watching your target. Convert cash to AKs, weed, beer — anything that doesn't show in NW — as fast as possible.

Advanced: Net Worth Management

At the top level, NW isn't just a score — it's a tool you actively shape. Here's how experienced pimps think about it:

Hiding wealth: Cash, crack, thugs, and rides all add to your visible NW. AKs, weed, beer, condoms, and Kevlar Vests do not. When you have more cash than you need for operations, convert it into invisible assets. A pimp who looks broke on the rankings is a pimp who doesn't get ganked.

Diving: Sell thugs or crack to deliberately lower your NW and slide into attack range of a target who'd otherwise be out of reach. This is a precision tool — calculate exactly how far you need to drop. Buy back immediately after the hit.

Reading other pimps' NW: When you see someone's NW jump suddenly, they just turned tricks. That's cash sitting on their pimp, and it'll disappear in seconds as they spend it. The speed of that jump is your gank window. The bigger the jump relative to their thug count, the more naked they are.

The payout trick: Flipping your payout temporarily to 100% makes your hoes happy with almost no crack. This frees up crack you were holding (or lets you hold less), shrinking your visible NW without actually giving anything up. Experienced pimps flip to 100% when they want to look smaller without actually selling off assets.

Deadnet awareness: Always know your own deadnet — what your NW would be if all your thugs died. That's the minimum you'd be worth if someone zeroed you. Keeping your deadnet modest (few rides, modest crack) makes you a less attractive target after a loss.

Never tell anyone what you're holding. Saying in chat that you're stocked with 500k crack is an invitation to be robbed. Keep your cards close.

Rounds

Rounds last 7 days. When the timer hits zero, the pimp with the highest net worth wins and their name goes into the Hall of Fame. Every round is a fresh start — nobody carries their thugs, hoes, cash, or supplies into the next round.

What does carry over: your account, your pimp name (you can change it each round if you want), your lifetime win count, your contact list (friends, enemies, ignored players), and your profile bio. The community and the rivalries persist even as the game resets.

At the start of each round you'll see an activation screen. Choose your city and name, confirm your password if you want to change it, and you're in. Your starting state is always the same: $4,500, 1 hoe, 1 thug, 4,000 turns, 1 token.

Between rounds you can still browse the rankings and round history — including full final standings and everyone's stats from any past round. Check the Round History page to study how top players built their rounds, and the Hall of Fame to see who's won before.

The round doesn't wait for you. By the time you're reading this, the clock is already running. Go pimp.
